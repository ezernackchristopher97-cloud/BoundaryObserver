\section{Related work}
\label{sec:relatedwork}

The paper sits at the intersection of several traditions. The comparison must remain exact: neighboring work motivates questions and supplies established results, but it does not become evidence for the new theorem merely because it uses similar words.

\subsection{Appearance, causation, and the conditions of representation}

Plato's cave is an early model of mediated appearance: the experienced display is real as experience without exhausting the structure that produces it \parencite{Plato1992}. Aristotle's analysis of cause warns against treating one explanatory relation as a complete account of an object \parencite{Aristotle1984}. Husserl's phenomenology examines how objects of science are constituted relative to intentional structures and a prior lifeworld \parencite{Husserl1970}. The present analysis does not adopt Platonic forms, Aristotelian metaphysics, or transcendental phenomenology. These sources clarify why the paper separates source state, accessible representation, explanatory level, and ontology.

\subsection{Leibnizian perspective, sufficient reason, and indiscernibility}

Leibniz is a central precursor. In the \emph{Monadology} and related essays, individual monads express one world from distinct points of view; his principle of sufficient reason asks what grounds the selection of one account rather than another; and the identity of indiscernibles asks whether genuinely distinct entities can share all properties \parencite{Leibniz1989,LeibnizClarke2000}. The present interface question reverses and restricts the last question. It asks whether states can remain distinct while sharing every property accessible through a declared interface. The answer is yes whenever the interface has a nontrivial fiber. This is not a refutation of Leibniz because ``all accessible predicates'' is weaker than ``all properties.'' It is a formal statement of why the distinction matters.

Leibniz's project of a universal characteristic and a calculus of reasoning is also relevant to machine-checked formalization. Lean is not a realization of that full project, but the aspiration to separate symbolic derivation from informal persuasion belongs to the same historical line.

\subsection{Process, relation, and hidden order}

Whitehead treats reality as process and relational becoming rather than a collection of independently fixed substances \parencite{Whitehead1978}. Bohm develops wholeness and implicate order, and Bohm with Hiley presents an ontological interpretation of quantum theory in which accessible phenomena are related to a deeper undivided process \parencite{Bohm1980,BohmHiley1993}. These sources motivate attention to transformations and preserved relations. The present analysis does not assume process metaphysics or Bohmian quantum theory. Its verified claims hold for ordinary sets and functions.

\subsection{Diagonalization, self-reference, and semantic closure}

Cantor's diagonal method established a general technique for producing an object outside a proposed enumeration \parencite{Cantor1891}. Russell exposed a contradiction in unrestricted self-membership \parencite{Russell1908}. G\"odel proved incompleteness for sufficiently expressive formal theories \parencite{Godel1931}. Tarski proved limits on internally defined truth predicates \parencite{Tarski1936}. Lawvere expressed a diagonal fixed-point schema categorically \parencite{Lawvere1969}. Hofstadter explored recursive self-description and strange loops as an explanatory pattern spanning formal systems and minds \parencite{Hofstadter1979,Hofstadter2007}. Penrose used G\"odelian arguments to motivate stronger claims about mind and computation \parencite{Penrose1989,Penrose1994}; those stronger conclusions remain disputed and are not assumed here.

The present analysis does not derive any of these theorems from an observation map, and they do not prove that a physical observer must have a noninjective interface. Their relevance is structural: they distinguish local formal limitations from unrestricted claims about reality and show why a general embedded-boundary invariant requires more than analogy.

\subsection{Cybernetics, self-production, and observing systems}

Wiener placed communication, control, and feedback in one mathematical program \parencite{Wiener1948}. Ashby treated regulation and requisite variety as relations between disturbances and available responses \parencite{Ashby1956}. Second-order cybernetics included the observer within the act of observing \parencite{VonFoerster1981}. Autopoietic theory emphasized organizational closure and the difference between a system's own organization and an external description \parencite{MaturanaVarela1980}. Rosen's relational biology asked how living organization differs from a merely external mechanism \parencite{Rosen1991}. Von Neumann's work on measurement and self-reproducing automata supplies an additional bridge between description, observer participation, and self-maintaining organization \parencite{VonNeumann1955,VonNeumann1966}.

These traditions motivate the embedded-observer problem. They do not provide the state-level observable quotient, model-level empirical quotient, and hidden-extension theorem sequence developed here.

\subsection{Observability, identifiability, and inverse problems}

Kalman observability concerns recovery of a dynamical state from an output trajectory in a system with specified dynamics \parencite{Kalman1963}. Hermann and Krener extended observability analysis to nonlinear systems \parencite{HermannKrener1977}. Structural identifiability asks whether distinct parameter values or model structures produce the same idealized observations \parencite{BellmanAstrom1970,Rothenberg1971,WalterPronzato1997}. Applied work distinguishes structural nonidentifiability from practical uncertainty due to noise or finite data \parencite{Calderon1980,Raue2009,Chis2011,Villaverde2016,Meshkat2009,Guillaume2019}.

Hadamard's well-posedness conditions and later regularization theory distinguish nonexistence, nonuniqueness, and instability \parencite{Hadamard1923,Tikhonov1963,Engl1996}. The present result isolates nonuniqueness at the interface level. Regularization may select a representative, but selection is not proof that the observation uniquely determines it.

\subsection{Statistical experiments, automata, and causal equivalence}

Blackwell comparison orders statistical experiments by informativeness for decision problems \parencite{Blackwell1951,Blackwell1953,Torgersen1991}. Moore experiments and bisimulation compare states or systems by designated external behavior \parencite{Moore1956,Park1981,Milner1989,LarsenSkou1991,Rutten2000}. Causal graphical models admit observational Markov-equivalence classes that can be refined by intervention or additional assumptions \parencite{VermaPearl1990,Chickering1995,Andersson1997,Pearl2009,HauserBuhlmann2012}.

The present formalization uses a deterministic reachable-set relation rather than probability kernels, utilities, transition traces, or causal independences. The common lesson is that equivalence is relative to a specified family of observations or tests.

\subsection{Latent representation learning and abstraction}

Finite mixtures, multilinear decompositions, and modern latent-variable models require explicit uniqueness conditions \parencite{Teicher1963,Kruskal1977,Allman2009,Anandkumar2014}. Unsupervised disentanglement is not identifiable in general without inductive bias \parencite{Locatello2019}, while auxiliary variables, multi-view structure, or stronger restrictions can restore identifiability \parencite{Hyvarinen2019,Khemakhem2020,Gresele2020,XiBloemReddy2023}. Tenenbaum and colleagues frame learning as the growth of structured abstractions rather than direct copying of sensory data \parencite{Tenenbaum2011}. The hidden-factor construction supplies a deterministic witness for the broad warning that latent semantics do not follow from observational agreement alone.

\subsection{Information, coarse-graining, constraint, and scale}

Information theory separates a source from its transmitted representation and studies what processing preserves or reduces \parencite{Shannon1948a,Shannon1948b,KullbackLeibler1951,CoverThomas2006}. Nonequilibrium self-organization, complex adaptive systems, and constraint-based emergence provide different accounts of how stable organization can persist across scales \parencite{NicolisPrigogine1977,Kauffman1993,Deacon2011,Mitchell2009}. Recent scale-free active-inference work constructs hierarchical generative models in which coarse-grained states and paths are recursively composed across levels \parencite{Friston2025ScaleFree}.

These approaches are directly relevant to preservation, re-encoding, compression, and dispersion. The present formalization defines none of their thermodynamic, probabilistic, or dynamical quantities. A future connection must specify the map and prove which invariants descend through it.

\subsection{Axiomatics, law, and invariance}

Hilbert's axiomatic geometry demonstrates how a mathematical structure can be characterized by relations that admit different realizations \parencite{Hilbert1902}. Newton's laws and Einstein's special relativity provide distinct models of physical law and invariance across admissible descriptions \parencite{Newton1999,Einstein1905}. Feynman's lectures on physical law emphasize that compact mathematical structure is not a substitute for empirical testing \parencite{Feynman1965}. These examples motivate the search for invariants across interfaces while guarding against the inference that every formal invariant is physically realized.

\subsection{Observer participation in physics and computation}

Wheeler's ``law without law'' program places observation and participatory description near the foundations of physics \parencite{Wheeler1983}. Constructor theory reframes physical laws in terms of possible and impossible transformations, including information-bearing transformations \parencite{Deutsch2013,DeutschMarletto2015}. Tegmark asks whether consciousness can be characterized as a state of matter with specified information-processing properties \parencite{Tegmark2015}. Wolfram's computational program emphasizes computational irreducibility and develops an explicit observer theory in which bounded observers aggregate computational histories \parencite{Wolfram2002,Wolfram2023ObserverTheory,RicklesEtAl2023}.

These programs differ sharply in ontology and mathematics. They are included because each makes the observer, transformation, or coarse-grained description an explicit part of the explanatory object. The present formalization does not infer physical law from computation and does not treat observer equivalence as evidence for any one foundational physics.


\subsection{Physical computation and heterogeneous readout}

Shannon's theory of the differential analyzer treats continuous physical evolution as an analog calculation \parencite{Shannon1941}. Horsman and colleagues distinguish the physical device from the abstract computation by an explicit representation relation \parencite{Horsman2014,Horsman2015}. Quantum computation adds a further separation among state preparation, controlled evolution, quantum measurement, and classical output \parencite{DiVincenzo2000,AharonovCotlerQi2022}. Brain--computer interfaces translate selected neural activity into device commands and require adaptation across the biological and computational sides of the interface \parencite{Wolpaw2002,Willett2023}.

These cases support a general caution: two devices may compute the same abstract function while differing in substrate, accessible state, noise, resources, and readout. Conversely, two interfaces may produce the same report without implementing the same process. The theorem concerns the declared observation relation and therefore applies only after the encoding and readout maps have been stated.

\subsection{Consciousness theories, interfaces, and current empirical tests}

Chalmers distinguishes functional explanation from the explanatory problem of subjective experience \parencite{Chalmers1995}. Integrated information theory, global workspace approaches, attention schema theory, resonant neural dynamics, predictive processing, and active inference offer different accounts of access, integration, report, or experience \parencite{Tononi2008,Koch2019,Graziano2013,Grossberg2021,Friston2010,Parr2022}. Jaynes provides a historically influential, controversial account of changes in self-modeling and source attribution \parencite{Jaynes1976}. Determinist and compatibilist accounts of agency frame a related level-of-description dispute: Sapolsky emphasizes the causal history of biological action, whereas List, Dennett, and Frankfurt defend forms of agency that do not require exemption from physical causation \parencite{Sapolsky2023,List2019,Dennett2003,Frankfurt1971}. Hoffman and Prakash propose interface theory and conscious-agent models in which perceptual representations do not transparently reproduce objective structure \parencite{HoffmanPrakash2014,PrentnerHoffman2024}.

Recent debate has emphasized explicit theory comparison and the danger of pseudo-operationalization \parencite{GomezMarinSeth2025,TononiEtAl2025}. The 2025 COGITATE adversarial collaboration preregistered divergent predictions of integrated information theory and global neuronal workspace theory and found results that challenged central predictions of both \parencite{Cogitate2025}. Laukkonen, Friston, and Chandaria propose a recursively shared world model and precision control as conditions for consciousness \parencite{Laukkonen2025}. Whyte and colleagues formulate a minimal testable consciousness theory implicit in active inference \parencite{Whyte2026}. Robinson and colleagues report an adversarial test design for an active-inference account of conscious content \parencite{Robinson2025}.

The present theorem does not prove a theory of consciousness. It supplies a reconstruction and equivalence language in which claims about unified state, reportability, hidden mechanism, and competing implementations can be separated.

\subsection{Artificial systems and machine consciousness}

Chalmers analyzes obstacles and possible future routes to consciousness in large language models \parencite{Chalmers2023LLM}. Butlin and colleagues derive computational indicator properties from several neuroscientific theories and use them to structure assessment of artificial systems \parencite{ButlinEtAl2023}. These works are relevant to the embedded-interface question because an artificial system may expose reports, hidden states, and self-monitoring channels that are neither identical nor mutually reconstructable. The present theorem does not decide whether any artificial system is conscious. It states what cannot be inferred from equality at a selected output interface alone.

\subsection{Recent generalized observers and self-referential living systems}

Elshatlawy and colleagues propose a cross-disciplinary formal theory of observers with operational observer equivalences and complexity measures \parencite{Elshatlawy2025}. Abramsky and colleagues identify time, self-reference, and self-modification as open formal problems for living systems and discuss domain theory, coalgebra, and self-modifying computation as possible tools \parencite{Abramsky2025}. Both are developing programs rather than settled results.

Their relevance is direct. The next formal extension must distinguish an external analyst from an embedded observer and model a self-interface whose outputs feed back into the system that generates them.

\subsection{Neural manifolds and reportable structure}

Toroidal organization in grid-cell population activity supplies a concrete example of structure recovered through a long measurement and reconstruction chain rather than observed without mediation \parencite{Gardner2022}. Related work has recovered toroidal organization in one-dimensional behavior and investigated how oscillatory dynamics contribute to the observed topology \parencite{Hermansen2024,DiSarra2025}. In each case, electrodes, sampling, spike sorting, behavioral variables, preprocessing, dimensionality reduction, and topological analysis participate in the accessible representation.

The relevance is methodological. A neural manifold can be a stable and informative empirical structure without being identical to the complete neural or experiential state that produced it. Future analysis should declare each map in the data-to-representation chain, test whether the recovered topology survives justified changes in preprocessing and sampling, and determine which latent alternatives remain observationally equivalent.

\subsection{Ultraweak photon emission and the light hypothesis}

Ultraweak photon emission is a measurable phenomenon associated with oxidative and metabolic processes in living systems. Recent work reports spontaneous emission from DNA under controlled conditions and measurable differences between living and dead mice, with stress-related changes in plants \parencite{PietruszkaMarzec2024,Salari2025}. Reviews summarize detection methods and proposed biological applications \parencite{Mould2024}.

These results do not establish that light is a universal informational standard of life, that biological information is conserved after death in an observer-independent sense, or that photon emission carries the complete organization of an organism. They identify a concrete observable that can enter a future scale-transition study without turning the motivating hypothesis into a present conclusion.

\subsection{Formal verification and encoded proof}

Lean provides a dependent-type-theory environment in which definitions and proofs can be checked mechanically \parencite{deMoura2015}. Mathlib supplies reusable formal mathematics \parencite{Mathlib2020}, and projects such as Flyspeck demonstrate the role of mechanical checking in substantial proofs \parencite{Hales2017}. The historical aspiration to mechanize valid reasoning also recalls Leibniz's universal-characteristic program \parencite{Leibniz1989}.

Mechanical checking certifies a relation between encoded premises, inference rules, and conclusions. It does not make the proof checker an unrestricted observer of physical reality, and it does not imply that classical symbolic logic, analog dynamics, quantum measurement, neural computation, and human reasoning share one representation interface. Physical-computation frameworks explicitly separate an abstract computational description from the device-level encoding and readout that realize it \parencite{Horsman2014,Horsman2015}. The distinction is therefore the same one used throughout the paper: correctness within an interface is not identity with every latent process that may implement, interpret, or motivate it.

\subsection{Distinct contribution}

The mathematical contribution is the combination of two quotient levels and an explicit countermodel construction. At the state level, an interface creates fibers and an observable quotient. At the model level, equality of reachable sets creates an empirical quotient. Hidden extensions move within one empirical class while changing latent structure and exact-decoder status. Interface refinement orders boundaries by the distinctions they preserve. The synthesis theorem packages these relations into one verified statement. The wider genealogy explains why that bounded result matters, but no historical or contemporary theory is treated as having proved it in advance.
